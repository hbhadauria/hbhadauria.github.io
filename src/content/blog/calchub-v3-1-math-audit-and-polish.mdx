## Auditing AI-written financial math

Shipping fast in `v1.0.0` proved that AI can scaffold 30 financial calculators overnight. It also left behind a classic AI-prototyping risk: formulas that look plausible on happy-path inputs but drift on regional conventions or edge cases.

Before letting an agent add a single new feature for `v3.1.0`, I ran a line-by-line audit of all 30 existing objects in `domain/logic/`. That pass uncovered **8 real calculation issues**:

- **GST (Inclusive)**: returned the tax fraction cleanly against the true pre-tax base rather than approximating from the gross figure.
- **Flat vs. Reducing**: compared identical quoted interest rates fairly across flat-rate and reducing-balance loans.
- **SWP**: stopped summing monthly withdrawals once the corpus depleted and handled the partial final payout.
- **RD**: switched from monthly to Indian bank-standard quarterly compounding.
- **SSY**: capped deposits at 15 years while continuing to compound through the 21-year maturity window.
- **EPF**: accrued monthly balances and credited interest annually.
- **APY**: aligned monthly contribution tables with official PFRDA slabs for entry ages 18–40 across all five pension tiers (₹1K–₹5K).
- **Income Tax**: added Section 87A rebate and 4% cess to the Old Regime, and moved the FY26 New Regime calculations out of UI screens into `IncomeTaxCalculator`.

  Audit every domain calculator against Indian financial rules, extract UI-inline formulas (`StepUpSipCalculator`, `LoanScheduleCalculator`, `IncomeTaxCalculator.calculateFy26NewRegime`), guard against zero, negative, `0%` rate, `NaN`, and `Infinity` inputs, and lock each fix with regression tests.
</AgentStep>

On top of those fixes, I added an optional **Adjust for Inflation** toggle (`0–15%`, defaulting to `6%`) inside SIP, Step-up SIP, and Lumpsum so users can discount projected maturity values to today's purchasing power.

## Scaling from 34 to 42 calculators with a spec-driven pattern

Once the domain contracts, edge-case guards, and unit-test conventions were locked, adding new calculators stopped being ad-hoc prompt engineering. An agent could follow the exact repository pattern to implement **8 new offline calculators** (bringing CalcHub from `34` to `42` tools, with `14` `Popular` picks), plus an upgraded **Salary** calculator with an instant CTC-to-in-hand FY26 New Regime mode:

1. **Capital Gains Tax (FY26)** — STCG/LTCG estimates across asset holding periods.
2. **Goal SIP Planner** — reverse-calculates the monthly SIP needed to hit a target corpus.
3. **Loan Prepayment Savings** — quantifies tenure reduction and interest saved from lump-sum or recurring prepayments.
4. **Credit Card Payoff** — models minimum-due traps versus fixed monthly payments.
5. **FIRE / Coast-FIRE** — calculates Financial Independence corpus targets and Coast-FIRE milestones.
6. **Buy vs. Rent** — compares net wealth from home ownership against renting and investing the surplus.
7. **Spend vs. Invest** — shows the long-term opportunity cost of discretionary purchases.
8. **Freelance Tax (Section 44ADA)** — estimates presumptive tax liability at 50% taxable profit under FY26 New Regime slabs.

To keep 42 tools fast to scan, `v3.1.0` also added a **Popular** filter orb on Home, starter preset chips, expandable plain-English financial term hints (`FinanceTermTooltip`), and one-tap offline result sharing via Android's `Intent.ACTION_SEND` sheet.

Like `v3.0.0`, new visual work started outside Kotlin: **Minimal Black** (`CalcHubSkin.MINIMAL_BLACK`, pure AMOLED contrast) was prototyped as an HTML comparison mockup rendered in headless Chrome before modifying theme tokens. `v3.1.0` also scoped background gradients strictly to Aurora, moved to a floating bottom navigation bar, gave Tools Lab an underline tab bar, and added an **About CalcHub** dialog.

The bigger AI lesson, though, came from copy-paste drift. Generating dozens of Compose screens quickly creates repeated scaffolds, result cards, and currency formatters. Instead of reviewing 42 screens manually, I pointed **PMD CPD** (copy-paste detection) and **`detekt` / SonarQube** complexity rules at the codebase and used those findings directly as the agent's refactoring target.

<DecisionRecord id="ADR-04" title="Calculator layouts" decision="Result-first ordering on shared Compose scaffolds">
  All 42 calculator screens now follow `Result -> Inputs -> Charts` backed by `rememberSaveable` state, and share `CalculatorScrollScaffold`, `StandardInvestmentCalculatorLayout`, and `StandardResultsCalculatorLayout`. Static-analysis-driven refactoring removed roughly 950 net lines of duplicated boilerplate and cut cross-file duplicate blocks from 122 to 79 (with zero duplication hits left in the refactored screens).
</DecisionRecord>

## Local pre-push gates & CI hardening

<AgentStep goal="QUALITY & CI GATES" phase="v3.1.0" result="41 suites / 116 JVM + Robolectric tests · 4/4 on-device Espresso + Compose · 0 lint errors · hardened workflows">
  Run local static analysis and workflow audits (`detekt`, PMD CPD, `actionlint`, `zizmor`) alongside JVM/Robolectric tests and real-device instrumentation checks before pushing PR #33.
</AgentStep>

Key CI and maintainability changes from `v3.0.0` to `v3.1.0` include:

- **Automated complexity cleanup**: introduced `FireInput` and `BuyVsRentInput` parameter objects and extracted helper composables to clear SonarQube parameter-count and cognitive-complexity checks.
- **PR validation**: wired `pull_request` triggers on `main` for unit tests, `lintDebug`, and `assembleDebug`, and disabled failing PR comment posts from the test reporter.
- **Least-privilege workflows**: locked top-level `permissions: {}` across workflows, disabled persisted checkout credentials, removed inline expression interpolation in shell blocks, and separated GitHub release packaging into `github-release.yml`.
- **Release signing & symbols**: enabled native debug symbols in release bundles and environment-driven release signing.
